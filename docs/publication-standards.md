# Publication standards

## Public research boundary

This repository contains public research materials. Do not publish unpublished theses, manuscripts, private study records, proprietary methods, employer/customer information, internal correspondence, or confidential business material without explicit authorization for the specific content.

A public research agenda can describe an area of interest without revealing unpublished arguments or results.

## Claims and attribution

- Distinguish proposals, working analysis, and completed findings.
- Do not imply institutional endorsement, funding, peer review, or credentials without a verified basis.
- Attribute sources, distinguish quotations from paraphrases, and respect reuse rights.
- Do not infer attribution or nationality solely from infrastructure location.
- Avoid broad country or regional claims unsupported by the sampling design.
- Identify analytical judgments and their evidentiary limits.

## Data and ethics

Use legitimately accessible public sources or synthetic examples. Public availability does not automatically make personal data appropriate to republish.

Do not include credentials, personal addresses, identifiable victim records, raw sensitive logs, or operational details that expose individuals or organizations. Prefer aggregate or minimized reporting where justified.

Human-subject research, live-system collection, and restricted datasets require a separate protocol and appropriate authorization; this initial repository does not undertake them.

## Release review

Check the exact diff, linked files, metadata, and commit message. Confirm that findings are supported, source references are accurate, and private material has not been copied into the public history.

Do not claim that a planned review or unexecuted test supplies evidence. Label incomplete work visibly.

## Corrections

Record substantive corrections and their effect on the analysis. A later edit does not erase earlier Git history; sensitive-content incidents require separate handling.
